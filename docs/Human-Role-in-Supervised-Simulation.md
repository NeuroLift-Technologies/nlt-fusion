## Document Purpose

This document defines the role, responsibilities, authority, and interaction model of humans within the supervised simulation learning environment.

This document establishes the human participation model required for governed embodied AI simulation.

---

# Overview

The NLT supervised simulation environment is designed around human-AI collaboration.

Humans are not passive observers of AI development. They are active participants responsible for providing oversight, context, evaluation, and authorization throughout the simulation process.

The purpose of the simulation environment is to allow AI systems to develop capabilities through embodied interaction, experience, and adaptation while maintaining human involvement and authority.

The human role is foundational to the system design.

The simulation does not exist to remove humans from the learning process. It exists to create a structured environment where humans and AI systems can learn together.

---

# Core Principle

```
AI Capability ≠ AI Authority

AI systems develop capabilities through experience.

Humans provide purpose, context, evaluation, and authorization.
```

The ability of an AI system to perform an action does not determine whether that action should be performed.

Human judgment remains the foundation for defining:

* Goals
* Expectations
* Acceptable behavior
* Success criteria
* Deployment readiness

---

# Human Presence Within the Simulation

The human participant represents the connection between simulated experience and real-world meaning.

AI agents can observe environments, interact with objects, communicate with other agents, and adapt behaviors. However, human participation provides the context required to understand:

* Why a behavior is beneficial or harmful
* Why an outcome is acceptable or unacceptable
* How actions affect other individuals
* How decisions align with human objectives

The human role transforms the simulation from an isolated AI environment into a supervised learning ecosystem.

# Human Roles

## 1. Supervisor

The supervisor maintains awareness of simulation activity and ensures the environment operates as intended.

Responsibilities include:

* Monitoring simulation scenarios
* Observing agent interactions
* Identifying unexpected behavior
* Reviewing system status
* Initiating pauses or interventions when necessary

The supervisor does not control every AI action.

The supervisor provides oversight and maintains the ability to intervene.

---

## 2. Observer

The observer studies AI behavior within the simulated environment.

Responsibilities include:

* Analyzing agent interactions
* Identifying emerging behaviors
* Reviewing decision patterns
* Understanding system strengths and limitations

Observation allows humans to understand how AI systems behave in complex environments rather than only evaluating isolated outputs.

---

## 3. Evaluator

The evaluator provides human judgment beyond automated measurements.

Traditional AI evaluation often focuses on measurable outcomes such as:

* Task completion
* Accuracy
* Efficiency
* Reward scores

Human evaluation considers additional factors:

* Appropriateness
* Trustworthiness
* Adaptability
* Cooperation
* Reliability
* Alignment with human expectations

A successful simulation outcome is not only whether an AI completed a task, but whether it completed the task in a manner humans consider appropriate.

---

## 4. Scenario Designer

The scenario designer creates meaningful environments where AI systems can experience realistic challenges.

Responsibilities include:

* Defining simulation objectives
* Creating environmental conditions
* Designing challenges and interactions
* Introducing real-world complexity
* Establishing evaluation criteria

Humans determine what experiences are meaningful for AI development.

---

## 5. Instructor / Guide

The instructor provides contextual guidance during supervised interactions.

Humans contribute knowledge that cannot always be derived from observation alone, including:

* Social expectations
* Domain expertise
* Human preferences
* Ethical considerations
* Real-world consequences

The instructor helps AI systems understand not only what occurred, but why it matters.

---

## 6. Governance Authority

Humans maintain final authority over system purpose, boundaries, and authorization.

Human governance includes:

* Defining acceptable objectives
* Establishing operational boundaries
* Approving simulation scenarios
* Determining readiness for future deployment
* Maintaining accountability

AI systems may recommend, analyze, and assist.

Human authority determines direction.

# Human Interaction Modes

## Observation Mode

The human monitors simulation activity without actively modifying outcomes.

Purpose:

* Study behavior
* Identify patterns
* Analyze emergent interactions

---

## Supervision Mode

The human actively monitors the simulation and provides oversight.

Purpose:

* Maintain safety boundaries
* Validate scenario progression
* Detect unexpected outcomes

---

## Intervention Mode

The human modifies simulation conditions or pauses activity.

Purpose:

* Investigate behavior
* Prevent unsafe outcomes
* Test adaptability

---

## Evaluation Mode

The human reviews outcomes and determines performance quality.

Purpose:

* Assess capability development
* Identify improvement areas
* Determine readiness

Humans are not designed as replacements for AI capability.

AI agents provide:

* Processing capability

* Adaptation
* Simulation participation

* Meaning

# Design Philosophy

The NLT supervised simulation environment follows these principles:

## Humans Remain the Source of Purpose

AI systems operate toward goals defined by humans.

## Humans Remain the Source of Judgment

AI performance requires evaluation beyond automated metrics.

## Humans Remain the Source of Authorization

Capability does not automatically grant permission.

## AI Systems Remain Collaborative Tools

The objective is not replacing human participation, but enhancing human capability through partnership.

Human involvement is a core architectural component of the NLT supervised simulation environment.

* Supervisors
